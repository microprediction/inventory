\medskip
I am submitting the enclosed manuscript, \emph{On a Simple Relationship Between Order Imbalance, Skew and Width in Over-The-Counter Trading}, for consideration as a \textbf{Short Communication} (9 pages).

The paper studies a market maker who trades only by responding to sealed-bid enquiries arriving with imbalanced intent. Its main result is an exact equivalence: in the steady state, the problem with arrival imbalance $q$ compresses onto the perfectly balanced problem under a translation of the skew by $\tfrac{w}{2}\log\tfrac{q}{1-q}$, a widening of the quotes, and a multiplication of the carrying cost by $1/(2\sqrt{q(1-q)})$, where $w$ is the observable market width. Consequences include a closed form for the skew a dealer should quote at zero inventory, the observation that skew responds to imbalance at first order while width responds at second order, and the location of the constant-width linear-skew benchmark of Avellaneda and Stoikov as a corner case. The exponential assumption on the best competing quote is shown to be needed only locally, and a short numerical certificate accompanies the paper as an ancillary script.

To my knowledge no equivalence of this kind appears in the optimal market making literature; the treatments of asymmetric arrival intensities in that line are numerical. The underlying work was completed around 2015 and the central symmetry was presented at NYU Tandon some time later, but the result has never been published. A companion paper with Andrew Papanicolaou extends the model in other directions and will cite the present one.

The manuscript is not published and is not under consideration at any other journal. A preprint was submitted to arXiv today and its identifier will follow with the announcement.
