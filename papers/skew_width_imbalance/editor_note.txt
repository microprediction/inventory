Between Order Imbalance, Skew and Width in Over-The-Counter Trading

Dear Editors,

In follow-up work we found that the statement of Theorem 1 in our submission
treats two equivalent representations of a single factor — the carrying-cost
multiplier M(q) and the overhead displacement gamma — as simultaneous
corrections. The core algebraic reduction in the proof remains valid; what required revision is the interpretive layer built on the
double-counted factor, most visibly the claim that the dealer's physical
width widens by gamma.

The attached corrected manuscript restates the theorem in the cost frame as
an equivalence of relative values and policies, with the quote map making
the physical-width conclusion explicit; replaces the width and zero-carry
consequences with the correct ones, including a closed small-skew formula
for the width response through the carry channel; makes the interiority,
symmetry, and uniqueness assumptions explicit; and clarifies that the
constant-width inversion of Section 4 is reduced-form unless its
integrability condition holds. The central symmetry emerges more cleanly in
the revision, as a skew translation composed with a uniform time change of
the inventory process. A new certificate suite accompanying the manuscript provides finite-lattice
implementation checks of the principal identities and approximations, with
every test configuration satisfying the theorem's interior-quote assumption;
it further verifies the algebraic identities exactly in a computer algebra
system, certifies finite-lattice optimality by an exact policy-iteration
fixed point, and proves existence, local uniqueness, and interiority of the
solution by interval-Newton methods, pointwise and for a continuum of
imbalance values at the certificate cost.

We preferred to send the corrected manuscript now rather than have referees
spend time on statements we know to be wrong, and we apologize for the
inconvenience.

Sincerely,
Peter Cotton
