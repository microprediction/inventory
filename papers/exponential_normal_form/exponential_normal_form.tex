\begin{abstract}
In the sealed-bid model of \citet{cotton2026skew}, exponential competing quotes make flow imbalance exactly removable: skew translated, quotes widened, carrying cost multiplied. This paper treats arbitrary competing-quote distributions. In coordinates built from the logarithm of the enquiry value, imbalance is an exact translation for every distribution. Rigid quote translations suffice precisely for the affine-exponential family $C + Be^{-hK}$, and for the exponential alone under the natural tail condition; outside it the pointwise removal fails to lift to a single inventory potential, the failure measured by a curvature-controlled integrability defect. A perturbation hierarchy built on one fixed linear operator computes the correction to the potential and to the quotes at every order, with standard distribution families entering through a single envelope evaluation. A distribution-free parity theorem shows the skew is odd and the width response even in the log-odds of the flow. All formulas and asymptotic orders are checked numerically.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} market making, order imbalance, skew, bid--ask spread, normal form, perturbation theory, request for quote.

\section{Introduction}

\citet{cotton2026skew} considers a market maker who acquires and disposes of inventory only by responding to sealed-bid enquiries, arriving as a Poisson stream in which a seller calls with probability $q$ and a buyer with probability $1-q$. Under the assumption that the best competing response is exponentially distributed beyond fair value with mean $w = 1/h$, the steady state of the imbalanced problem compresses exactly onto the balanced one: skew translated by $\delta = \tfrac{1}{2h}\log\tfrac{q}{1-q}$, non-discretionary width widened by $\gamma = \tfrac1h \log \tfrac{1}{2\sqrt{q(1-q)}}$, and carrying cost multiplied by $M(q) = e^{h\gamma} = 1/(2\sqrt{q(1-q)})$.

The exponential assumption invites the obvious question, and \citet{cotton2026skew} gave it a local answer: only the hazard at the quotes actually made enters, and if the hazard varies slowly across the visited strikes the equivalence holds approximately with the local width, with error of the order of the hazard's relative variation. That is a robustness statement. The present paper replaces it with a structure: an exponential \emph{normal form}, in which general win curves are deformations of an integrable anchor and every correction is computable.

The two organizing words are borrowed from neighboring fields, and the borrowing is the motivation. A \emph{normal form}, as in dynamical systems and control, is a canonical coordinate system in which a family of problems takes its simplest expression, together with a classification of which members become trivial there. The instructive example is feedback linearization: every controllable linear system is equivalent to a canonical form, and the geometric theory classifies which nonlinear systems the allowed transformations carry onto a linear one.

This paper does the same for imbalanced market making. There are coordinates, built from the logarithm of the value of an enquiry, in which imbalance is a pure translation for \emph{every} win curve; the classification question is then which win curves let that translation be realized by a \emph{rigid motion} of the physical quotes --- every strike sliding by the same constant, the way a ruler slides along a desk --- rather than by a gauge that bends the ladder, moving each strike by its own amount.

\emph{Rigidity} names the character of the answer. As in the memorylessness characterization of the exponential distribution, and in the older tradition of rigidity theorems in geometry where a weak hypothesis forces a short list, demanding a rigid motion collapses the infinite-dimensional family of win curves onto a two-parameter class, and onto the exponential itself once tails are imposed. Exponentiality is to this problem what linearizability is to control: not an approximation to be excused, but the distinguished member around which the general theory organizes itself. Away from the distinguished member the questions become quantitative --- an obstruction to measure, and a correction to compute --- and those are the remaining results.

The organizing observation is that after the dealer optimizes her own markup, the distribution of the best competing quote enters the steady state through one function only,
\[
G(K) \;=\; \sup_m\,(m-K)\,\Fbar(m), \qquad \Fbar = 1 - F,
\]
the optimized value of the next enquiry as a function of its strike $K$; quoting against the survival curve of the best competing bid in this way descends from \citet{friedman1956}. Throughout, \emph{win curve} means the survival function $\Fbar$ and $G$ is called the \emph{enquiry-value curve}; the two are related by the envelope calculus of Section~\ref{sec:setup} and Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible}. The right object to deform is not $\Fbar$ but the \emph{effective log-value}
\[
\Phi(K) \;=\; -\log\frac{G(K)}{G_\star},
\]
with $G_\star$ an arbitrary normalization. The envelope identity of \citet{cotton2026skew}, $-\tfrac{d}{dK}\log G(K) = h(m^\ast(K))$, says $\Phi$ is the integral of the effective hazard sampled along the optimal response, so exponential competing quotes correspond exactly to $\Phi$ affine, and $\Phi''$ is the natural local measure of non-exponentiality.

Five results organize the theory.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Normal form} (Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform}). For every smooth positive enquiry-value curve, in the nonlinear coordinates $(U,V)$ built from $\Phi$, imbalance is exactly a translation: by $\log M(q)$ in the width-like coordinate and by the log-odds $\ell = \tfrac12\log\tfrac{q}{1-q}$ in the skew-like coordinate. Exponentiality is not what creates the symmetry; it is what makes the coordinates affine, so that the translation is a rigid motion of the physical quotes.
\item \emph{Rigidity} (Theorems~\ref{thm:rigidity} and~\ref{thm:hamrigidity}). The sidewise gauge maps are translations only for exponential $G$; and, at the level that matters economically, rigid quote translations balance the two-sided enquiry Hamiltonian exactly for the affine-exponential family $G = C + Be^{-hK}$ and no other. The constant $C$ cancels from the consistency equation, so the compression of \citet{cotton2026skew} extends verbatim to that family; the tail condition $G(K) \to 0$, automatic for genuine win curves, then singles out the pure exponential.
\item \emph{Integrability defect} (Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}). Outside the rigid class the pointwise removal survives but generically fails to respect the constraint that both strike ladders derive from a single inventory potential; the exact criterion is that a defect functional be constant on the visited strike set. The defect's variation begins at first order in the curvature, with an explicit oscillation bound, and its \emph{constant part} at the affine point is exactly $2\gamma$: the widening of \citet{cotton2026skew} is the value of the defect functional at the integrable point.
\item \emph{Perturbation hierarchy} (Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}), \emph{envelope transfer} (Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope}), and \emph{admissibility} (Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible}). Writing $\Phi_\eta = a + hK + \eta\psi(K)$, where the fixed function $\psi$ carries the shape of the departure from exponentiality and $\eta$ its size (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), the inventory potential expands as $\nu_\eta = \nu_0 + \eta\nu_1 + \cdots$ where $\nu_0$ is the exact exponential solution and every $\nu_j$ solves a linear system with one fixed operator, tridiagonal up to a rank-one centering correction. Deformations of standard families enter through a single envelope evaluation at the exponential optimum --- for the Weibull family with shape $1+\eta$, $\psi(K) = (1+hK)\log(1+hK)$ --- so the theory is a functional expansion of the log-survival curve rather than one calculation per named distribution, and the admissibility proposition guarantees the deformations traversed correspond to genuine markup distributions. Corollary~\ref{cor:tangentpolicy} turns the value correction into the quote correction itself.
\item \emph{Parity} (Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}). Distribution-free: with a common win curve on both sides and even carrying cost, the skew contains only odd powers of $\ell$ and the width response only even powers. Under nondegeneracy of the leading coefficients the familiar reading follows --- skew first order, width second order --- and the exponential model supplies the all-orders coefficients $\delta = \ell/h$ and $\gamma = \tfrac1h\log\cosh\ell$, not the parity itself.
\end{enumerate}

The formulas and their predicted asymptotic orders are independently checked in the accompanying script \texttt{verify\_normal\_form.py}, at machine precision for the exact identities and by Richardson ratio tests for the expansions (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}).

\section{Setup: the effective log-value}\label{sec:setup}

The model is that of \citet{cotton2026skew}. Enquiries arrive with mean gap $\tau$; each is a sell with probability $q$, a buy with probability $1-q$; trades have size $s$; every won trade concedes $\epsilon$ to adverse selection; holding $x$ costs $c(x)$ per unit time with $c(0)=0$. The policy is characterized by an inventory indifference cost $\nu(x)$ with slope and convexity
\[
S(x) = \frac{\nu(x+s)-\nu(x-s)}{2s}, \qquad
C(x) = \frac{\nu(x+s)-2\nu(x)+\nu(x-s)}{2s},
\]
and break-even strikes
\begin{equation}
K^{\downarrow}(x) = \epsilon + \frac{\nu(x+s)-\nu(x)}{s} = A(x) + S(x), \qquad
K^{\uparrow}(x) = \epsilon + \frac{\nu(x-s)-\nu(x)}{s} = A(x) - S(x),
\label{eq:strikes}
\end{equation}
where $A = \epsilon + C$ collects the overhead and convexity. The best competing response beyond fair value has survival function $\Fbar$, assumed smooth and positive with $G(K) = \sup_m (m-K)\Fbar(m)$ finite, positive, and attained at an interior optimum $m^\ast(K)$ on the strike range of interest. The steady-state consistency equation is, for all $x$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\tau\,c(x)}{s} \;=\; H_q\big(A(x), S(x)\big) - H_q\big(A(0), S(0)\big),
\qquad
H_q(A,S) = q\,G(A+S) + (1-q)\,G(A-S).
\label{eq:consistency}
\end{equation}
For exponential $\Fbar(m) = e^{-hm}$ one has $G(K) = \tfrac1h e^{-1-hK}$ and \eqref{eq:consistency} is the equation solved in closed correspondence by \citet{cotton2026skew}.

Define the effective log-value $\Phi(K) = -\log(G(K)/G_\star)$. The envelope identity of \citet{cotton2026skew} gives
\begin{equation}
\Phi'(K) \;=\; h\big(m^\ast(K)\big) \;>\; 0,
\label{eq:envelope}
\end{equation}
so $\Phi$ is strictly increasing and invertible on its range: it is the integral of the effective hazard sampled at the optimal response. Three consequences of \eqref{eq:envelope} frame everything below:
exponential competing markups are exactly the case $\Phi(K) = a + hK$;
departures from exponentiality are exactly departures of $\Phi$ from affinity;
and $\Phi''$ is the natural local measure of non-exponentiality. Accordingly we write deformations of the anchor as
\begin{equation}
\Phi_\eta(K) \;=\; a + hK + \eta\,\psi(K),
\label{eq:deformation}
\end{equation}
and this is where $\psi$, the object the rest of the paper computes with, is defined: a fixed smooth function, the \emph{deformation}, carrying the shape of the departure from exponentiality, while the scalar $\eta$ carries its size. Constant and affine components of $\psi$ are absorbed into $a$ and $h$, and around an anchor strike $K_0$ one may normalize $\psi(K_0) = \psi'(K_0) = 0$, so that the perturbation genuinely begins with curvature. Section~\ref{sec:envelope} supplies $\psi$ from named distribution families; until then it is simply given.

\section{The normal form}\label{sec:normalform}

\begin{theorem}[Imbalance is a translation in the $\Phi$-coordinates]\label{thm:normalform}
Let $G$ be any positive function with $\Phi = -\log(G/G_\star)$ strictly increasing, and define
\[
U(A,S) = \frac{\Phi(A+S)+\Phi(A-S)}{2}, \qquad
V(A,S) = \frac{\Phi(A+S)-\Phi(A-S)}{2}, \qquad
\ell = \frac12\log\frac{q}{1-q}.
\]
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
H_q(A,S) \;=\; 2\sqrt{q(1-q)}\;G_\star\, e^{-U(A,S)}\cosh\big(V(A,S)-\ell\big),
\label{eq:normalform}
\end{equation}
and, writing $\mathcal H_q(U,V)$ for $H_q$ expressed through the coordinates $(U,V)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal H_q(U,V) \;=\; \mathcal H_{1/2}\big(U + \log M(q),\; V - \ell\big),
\qquad M(q) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{q(1-q)}} = \cosh\ell .
\label{eq:translation}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi(K^{\downarrow}) = U + V$ and $\Phi(K^{\uparrow}) = U - V$, so
$H_q = G_\star e^{-U}\big(q e^{-V} + (1-q)e^{V}\big)$, and the identity of \citet{cotton2026skew},
$q e^{-V} + (1-q) e^{V} = 2\sqrt{q(1-q)}\cosh(V - \ell)$ gives \eqref{eq:normalform}. Since $\mathcal H_{1/2}(U,V) = G_\star e^{-U}\cosh V$ and $2\sqrt{q(1-q)} = e^{-\log M(q)}$, \eqref{eq:translation} follows. That $M(q) = \cosh\ell$ is the elementary computation $\cosh\ell = \tfrac{q+(1-q)}{2\sqrt{q(1-q)}}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The theorem of \citet{cotton2026skew}, and why it is rigid there]\label{cor:companion}
For exponential competing quotes, $\Phi(K) = a + hK$, so $U = a + hA$ and $V = hS$ are affine and the translation \eqref{eq:translation} is a rigid motion of the quotes themselves:
\[
S \longmapsto S - \frac{\ell}{h} = S - \delta, \qquad
A \longmapsto A + \frac{\log M(q)}{h} = A + \gamma .
\]
Moreover $e^{-h(A+\gamma)} = e^{-h\gamma} e^{-hA}$ factors the widening through the equation, so it may be moved to the cost side of \eqref{eq:consistency} as the multiplier $M(q) = e^{h\gamma}$: for exponential $G$, widened overhead and inflated carrying cost are the same statement, at exchange rate $h$. Outside the affine-exponential family of Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity} no such factorization exists, and the widening --- not the multiplier --- is the invariant form of the correction.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What the theorem does and the coordinates hide]
Equation \eqref{eq:translation} holds for every smooth positive enquiry-value curve, so the imbalance symmetry as such costs nothing. What it does not by itself deliver is a solution map: the consistency equation couples the $(U,V)$ values at different inventories through the requirement that all strikes derive from one potential $\nu$, and the translation in $(U,V)$ moves each inventory's strike pair by a different physical amount unless $\Phi$ is affine. Sections~\ref{sec:rigidity} and~\ref{sec:defect} make that obstruction precise.
\end{remark}

\section{Two rigidity theorems}\label{sec:rigidity}

Pointwise, imbalance can always be removed. Define the gauge maps, for $\lambda > 0$,
\begin{equation}
T_\lambda(K) \;=\; \Phi^{-1}\big(\Phi(K) - \log \lambda\big),
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
G\big(T_\lambda(K)\big) = \lambda\,G(K).
\label{eq:gauge}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
H_q\big(A(x),S(x)\big) \;=\; \tfrac12\, G\big(T_{2q}(K^{\downarrow}(x))\big) + \tfrac12\, G\big(T_{2(1-q)}(K^{\uparrow}(x))\big):
\label{eq:pointwise}
\end{equation}
every imbalanced Hamiltonian is a balanced one evaluated at gauged strikes. For exponential $\Phi$ the gauge is the constant translation $T_\lambda(K) = K - \tfrac1h\log \lambda$; in general $T_\lambda(K) - K$ depends on $K$, and the exponential case is the only one in which it does not.

\begin{theorem}[Sidewise gauge rigidity]\label{thm:rigidity}
Let $G$ be positive and strictly decreasing with $\Phi$ continuously differentiable on an interval $I$, and suppose that for every $\lambda$ in some open interval around $1$ the gauge $T_\lambda$ restricted to $I$ is a translation: $T_\lambda(K) = K - t(\lambda)$ for a constant $t(\lambda)$. Then $\Phi'$ is constant on $I$: $G$ is exponential there.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$T_\lambda(K) = K - t(\lambda)$ means $G(K - t(\lambda)) = \lambda\,G(K)$ for all $K \in I$, i.e.\ $\Phi(K) - \Phi(K - t(\lambda)) = \log \lambda$ with the left side independent of $K$. Differentiating in $K$: $\Phi'(K) = \Phi'(K - t(\lambda))$ for all $K$ and every $t(\lambda)$. The map $\lambda \mapsto t(\lambda)$ is injective ($G$ is strictly decreasing), so the continuous function $\Phi'$ admits an uncountable set of periods; the period set of a continuous nonconstant function is a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb R$, hence countable. So $\Phi'$ is constant on $I$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is a statement about the two sidewise gauges separately. It does not by itself characterize the curves for which the \emph{summed} Hamiltonian can be balanced by rigid quote translations, and the distinction is not pedantic: the two-parameter family
\[
G(K) \;=\; C + B\,e^{-hK}, \qquad B > 0, \; C > 0,
\]
has curved $\Phi$, yet
\begin{align*}
H_q(A,S) &= C + B e^{-hA}\big(q e^{-hS} + (1-q)e^{hS}\big) \\
        &= C + 2B\sqrt{q(1-q)}\, e^{-hA}\cosh(hS - \ell)
        \;=\; H_{1/2}(A + \gamma,\, S - \delta):
\end{align*}
the constant passes through both sides and the exponential part translates rigidly.

Moreover $C$ cancels from the consistency equation \eqref{eq:consistency}, which sees only differences of $H_q$, so the entire compression of \citet{cotton2026skew} --- shift $\delta$, widening $\gamma$, multiplier $M(q)$ --- holds verbatim for this family; and the family is locally realizable by a genuine markup distribution, with optimizer $m^\ast(K) = K + \tfrac1h + \tfrac{C}{hB}e^{hK}$ and survival $\Fbar(m^\ast(K)) = -G'(K) = hBe^{-hK}$ on any interval where the latter is at most one. The affine-exponential family is therefore not a pathology but the exact boundary of rigid behavior:

\begin{theorem}[Hamiltonian rigidity]\label{thm:hamrigidity}
Let $G$ be twice continuously differentiable and strictly decreasing on an open interval $I$, and suppose that for each $q$ in a nondegenerate interval of imbalances there exist constants $\gamma(q)$ and $\delta(q)$ with
\[
H_q(A,S) \;=\; H_{1/2}\big(A + \gamma(q),\, S - \delta(q)\big)
\]
for all $(A,S)$ in an open set whose strikes $A \pm S$ range over $I$. Then $-G'$ satisfies the multiplicative translation property of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity}, hence is exponential, and
\[
G(K) \;=\; C + B\,e^{-hK} \qquad\text{on } I
\]
for some constants $C \in \mathbb R$, $B > 0$, $h > 0$. If in addition $G(K) \to 0$ as $K \to \infty$ --- automatic when $G$ is the enquiry value of a win curve with $\Fbar(m) = o(1/m)$ --- then $C = 0$ and $G$ is exponential.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the identity with respect to $S$ and with respect to $A$, and add:
\[
2q\,G'(A+S) \;=\; G'\big(A + \gamma + S - \delta\big),
\]
i.e.\ with $K = A + S$: $\;2q\,G'(K) = G'(K + \gamma(q) - \delta(q))$ for all $K$; subtracting instead gives $2(1-q)\,G'(K') = G'(K' + \gamma(q) + \delta(q))$ with $K' = A - S$. So the positive continuous function $-G'$ is carried to the multiple $2q$ of itself by the state-independent translation $\delta(q) - \gamma(q)$, for every $q$ in the range. These translations are injective in $q$, so applying the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} to $-\log(-G')$, which is continuously differentiable since $G$ is $C^2$, gives $(\log(-G'))'$ constant: $-G'(K) = hB\,e^{-hK}$. Integrating gives $G = C + Be^{-hK}$. The tail condition forces $C = 0$; and for a genuine win curve, $0 \le G(K) \le \sup_{m > K}(m-K)\Fbar(m) \to 0$ whenever $\Fbar(m) = o(1/m)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What each theorem governs]
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is the right tool for the defect functional of the next section, which is built from the sidewise gauges. Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity} is the right statement of the headline claim: among admissible enquiry-value curves, rigid quote translations remove imbalance exactly when $G$ is affine-exponential, and exponential once tails are imposed. The defect functional $D$ below measures the failure of the \emph{chosen termwise gauge} to lift to a potential; Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity} closes the remaining gap by ruling out every other rigid Hamiltonian-level balancing.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Provenance of the functional equation]
The core of Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} is the multiplicative Cauchy equation, and the conclusion is the lack-of-memory characterization of the exponential law, for which the systematic reference is \citet{galamboskotz1978} and the regularity machinery is \citet{aczel1966}; \citet{marsagliatubilla1975} show how far the continuum-of-shifts hypothesis can be weakened. What is not in that literature is the two-sided statement: Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity} lifts the characterization from the distribution to the pricing Hamiltonian, where the memoryless family acquires its affine extension.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Rigidity run backwards is identification]
A dealer who finds that sliding her quotes by a constant absorbs the flow has, by Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity}, learned her market's rejection curve up to two constants. The theorem's economic use is therefore not only to delimit when the formulas of \citet{cotton2026skew} are exact, but to turn an observed sufficiency of rigid skewing into a statement about the shape of the competition.
\end{remark}

\section{The integrability defect}\label{sec:defect}

The strikes \eqref{eq:strikes} are not free sequences: adjacent inventory states share the increment of $\nu$, which telescopes into the edge identity
\begin{equation}
K^{\downarrow}(x) + K^{\uparrow}(x+s) \;=\; 2\epsilon \qquad\text{for all } x .
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Reconstruction]\label{lem:edge}
A pair of sequences $\big(\tilde K^{\downarrow}(x), \tilde K^{\uparrow}(x)\big)$ derives from a single potential $\tilde\nu$ and overhead $\tilde\epsilon$ via \eqref{eq:strikes} if and only if $\tilde K^{\downarrow}(x) + \tilde K^{\uparrow}(x+s) = 2\tilde\epsilon$ for all $x$, in which case $\tilde\nu$ is recovered, up to an additive constant, by summing $\tilde\nu(x+s) - \tilde\nu(x) = s\,(\tilde K^{\downarrow}(x) - \tilde\epsilon)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Necessity is the computation \eqref{eq:edge}; sufficiency is the stated summation, whose consistency with the up-strikes is precisely the edge identity.
\end{proof}

After the pointwise gauge \eqref{eq:pointwise}, the transformed strikes are $\tilde K^{\downarrow}(x) = T_{2q}(K^{\downarrow}(x))$ and $\tilde K^{\uparrow}(x) = T_{2(1-q)}(K^{\uparrow}(x))$, and by \eqref{eq:edge} the reconstruction condition becomes: the \emph{defect functional}
\begin{equation}
D(K) \;=\; T_{2q}(K) + T_{2(1-q)}(2\epsilon - K)
\label{eq:defect}
\end{equation}
must be constant in $K$ across the visited strike set. That equivalence is exact and governs everything in this section: lifting occurs if and only if $D$ is constant on the strikes actually visited. Affinity of $\Phi$ guarantees it globally; curvature destroys it generically but not universally --- a curved $G$ can make $D$ accidentally constant on a particular finite ladder, and at $q = \tfrac12$ the obstruction vanishes trivially since both gauges are the identity.

\begin{theorem}[The defect measures curvature; its constant part is the widening]\label{thm:defect}
\emph{(i)} For affine $\Phi = a + hK$,
\[
D(K) \;\equiv\; 2\epsilon - \frac{\log\big(4q(1-q)\big)}{h} \;=\; 2\epsilon + 2\gamma :
\]
the defect is constant, the gauged problem is a balanced problem with potential $\tilde\nu(x) = \nu(x) - \delta x$ and overhead $\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon + \gamma$, and the widening of \citet{cotton2026skew} is the value of the defect functional at the integrable point.
\emph{(ii)} For the deformation \eqref{eq:deformation}, with $a_1 = \tfrac1h\log(2q)$ and $a_2 = \tfrac1h\log\big(2(1-q)\big)$ (so $a_1 + a_2 = -2\gamma$),
\begin{equation}
T_\lambda(K) \;=\; K - \frac{\log \lambda}{h} + \frac{\eta}{h}\Big[\psi(K) - \psi\Big(K - \frac{\log \lambda}{h}\Big)\Big] + O(\eta^2),
\label{eq:Tcexpansion}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
D(K) \;=\; 2(\epsilon+\gamma) + \frac{\eta}{h}\Big[\psi(K) - \psi(K - a_1) + \psi(2\epsilon - K) - \psi(2\epsilon - K - a_2)\Big] + O(\eta^2).
\label{eq:defectexpansion}
\end{equation}
Affine components of $\psi$ contribute a constant to \eqref{eq:defectexpansion} and no $K$-dependence: they renormalize the exponential width and overhead and create no genuine defect. The variation of $D$ therefore begins, at first order in $\eta$, with a second-difference functional of $\psi$ --- non-exponential curvature is the integrability defect of the local imbalance gauge.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Substitute $T_\lambda(K) = K - \tfrac1h\log \lambda$ into \eqref{eq:defect}; the $K$'s cancel and $\log(2q) + \log(2(1-q)) = \log(4q(1-q)) = -2h\gamma$. That $\tilde\nu = \nu - \delta x$ matches: $\tilde K^{\downarrow} = K^{\downarrow} - \tfrac1h\log 2q$ and $\epsilon + \gamma - \delta + \mathrm{D}^{+}\nu - \delta$ agree because $\gamma - \delta = \tfrac1h\log\tfrac{1}{2q}$.
(ii) Write $\Phi_\eta(T) = \Phi_\eta(K) - \log \lambda$ and expand $T = T^{(0)} + \eta T^{(1)} + O(\eta^2)$: at order zero $h(T^{(0)} - K) = -\log \lambda$; at order one $h T^{(1)} + \psi(T^{(0)}) - \psi(K) = 0$, giving \eqref{eq:Tcexpansion}; summing the two gauges per \eqref{eq:defect} gives \eqref{eq:defectexpansion}. For affine $\psi(K) = uK + v$ the bracket in \eqref{eq:defectexpansion} is $u(a_1 + a_2) = -2u\gamma$, a constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[A quantitative curvature bound]\label{cor:oscbound}
Write $B_\psi(K)$ for the bracket in \eqref{eq:defectexpansion}. Then $|B_\psi'(K)| \le (|a_1| + |a_2|)\,\|\psi''\|_\infty$, and on a visited strike interval $I$,
\[
\operatorname*{osc}_{K \in I} D(K) \;\le\; \frac{|\eta|}{h}\,\mathrm{diam}(I)\,\big(|a_1| + |a_2|\big)\,\|\psi''\|_\infty \;+\; O(\eta^2).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$B_\psi'(K) = \big[\psi'(K) - \psi'(K - a_1)\big] - \big[\psi'(2\epsilon - K) - \psi'(2\epsilon - K - a_2)\big]$, and each bracket is bounded by $|a_i|\,\|\psi''\|_\infty$ by the mean value theorem; integrate over $I$.
\end{proof}

The bound is the rigorous successor of the slowly-varying-hazard remark of \citet{cotton2026skew}: the defect, hence the size of the correction the hierarchy must supply, is controlled by the curvature of the effective log-value times the lengths $|a_1|, |a_2|$ of the gauge translations, which vanish at balance. It also supplies a natural robustness radius for the policy-learning applications discussed at the end.

\begin{remark}[The genre of the obstruction]
Conditions under which per-edge weights lift to a potential are the territory of reversibility theory --- Kolmogorov's criterion and its descendants \citep{kelly1979} --- where the obstruction lives on cycles. The inventory lattice is a line, so the obstruction here enters differently: through the pairing \eqref{eq:edge} of the up and down strikes across each bond, which the two sidewise gauges must respect jointly. The defect functional is the pricing-theoretic member of that family of discrete integrability conditions rather than a transplant of the cycle criterion.
\end{remark}

\section{The perturbation hierarchy}\label{sec:hierarchy}

The defect obstructs an exact compression, not a computation. Work on the inventory lattice $x \in \{-Ns, \dots, Ns\}$ with the normalization $\nu(0) = 0$, write $G_\eta = G_\star e^{-\Phi_\eta}$ with \eqref{eq:deformation}, and define the residual map
\begin{equation}
F_x(\nu, \eta) \;=\; q\,G_\eta\big(\epsilon + \mathrm{D}^{+}\nu(x)\big) + (1-q)\,G_\eta\big(\epsilon + \mathrm{D}^{-}\nu(x)\big) - \big[\,x \to 0\,\big] - \frac{\tau c(x)}{s},
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{D}^{\pm}\nu(x) = (\nu(x\pm s) - \nu(x))/s$ and $[\,x\to 0\,]$ denotes the same expression at $x = 0$, together with a boundary closure at the lattice ends; the closure used here and in the certificates sets the third difference to zero at each end,
\[
\nu(\pm N s) - 3\nu(\pm(N-1)s) + 3\nu(\pm(N-2)s) - \nu(\pm(N-3)s) \;=\; 0,
\]
extrapolating the convexity of the potential through the boundary. At $\eta = 0$ the exact solution is known from \citet{cotton2026skew}: $\nu_0 = \nu_{\mathrm{bal}} + \delta x$, the tilt of the balanced solve at cost $M(q)\,c$.

\begin{theorem}[One linear operator computes every order]\label{thm:hierarchy}
Let $K^{\downarrow}_0, K^{\uparrow}_0$ denote the strikes of $\nu_0$ and suppose the linearization
\begin{equation}
(L_0 u)(x) \;=\; h\,\Delta_0\Big[\, q\,G_0(K^{\downarrow}_0)\,\mathrm{D}^{+}u + (1-q)\,G_0(K^{\uparrow}_0)\,\mathrm{D}^{-}u \,\Big](x),
\qquad \Delta_0 f(x) = f(x) - f(0),
\label{eq:L0}
\end{equation}
closed with the boundary rows and the pinning $u(0)=0$, is nonsingular. If $G_\eta$ is $C^r$ jointly in $(K, \eta)$ on the relevant domain, then for small $\eta$ the consistency equation has a unique $C^r$ branch $\nu_\eta$, the expansion $\nu_\eta = \nu_0 + \eta\nu_1 + \cdots$ is valid through order $r$, and
\begin{equation}
L_0\,\nu_1 = -R_\psi,
\qquad
R_\psi(x) = \Delta_0\Big[\, q\,G_0(K^{\downarrow}_0)\,\psi(K^{\downarrow}_0) + (1-q)\,G_0(K^{\uparrow}_0)\,\psi(K^{\uparrow}_0) \,\Big](x),
\label{eq:firstorder}
\end{equation}
with, for every $2 \le j \le r$, $L_0\,\nu_j = R_j$, where $R_j$ is an explicit forcing built from lower-order data; in particular
\[
L_0 \nu_2 \;=\; \lim_{\eta\to 0}\frac{1}{\eta^{2}}\,F\big(\nu_0 + \eta \nu_1,\ \eta\big).
\]
If $G_\eta$ is real analytic in $(K,\eta)$, the branch is analytic and the series converges on a neighborhood of $\eta = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F(\nu_0, 0) = 0$ by the theorem of \citet{cotton2026skew}. The partial $\partial_\nu F(\nu_0, 0)$ is $-L_0$, since $F$ carries $+H_q$ and $\partial_K G_0 = -h\,G_0$; the partial $\partial_\eta F(\nu_0, 0)$ is $-R_\psi$, since $\partial_\eta G_\eta(K)\big|_{\eta=0} = -\psi(K)\,G_0(K)$. The implicit function theorem in the stated regularity class gives the branch, and $\nu_1 = -(\partial_\nu F)^{-1}\partial_\eta F$ is \eqref{eq:firstorder}. The displayed formula for $\nu_2$ is the order-$\eta^2$ term of $0 = F(\nu_\eta, \eta) = F(\nu_0 + \eta\nu_1, \eta) - \eta^2 L_0\nu_2 + O(\eta^3)$, and the same rearrangement produces each higher $R_j$ from residual evaluations along the already-computed truncation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Structure and cost of the hierarchy]
Before centering, the operator inside \eqref{eq:L0} is a birth--death tridiagonal operator with positive weights $qG_0(K^{\downarrow}_0)$ and $(1-q)G_0(K^{\uparrow}_0)$; the centering $\Delta_0$ subtracts one fixed linear functional of $u$ from every row, so $L_0$ is a tridiagonal matrix plus a rank-one correction --- equivalently, retaining the ergodic constant as an unknown instead of eliminating it through $\Delta_0$ gives a genuinely tridiagonal block with one border column. Either way it is factored once, for the fixed anchor problem (fixed $q$, cost, grid, and overhead), and every order of the expansion and every basis direction of Section~\ref{sec:envelope} is then one back-substitution; nothing nonlinear is ever re-solved. The certificate factors $L_0$ once and verifies, on a Weibull-born deformation, $\|\nu_\eta - \nu_0 - \eta\nu_1\|_\infty = O(\eta^2)$ and $\|\nu_\eta - \nu_0 - \eta\nu_1 - \eta^2\nu_2\|_\infty = O(\eta^3)$, with observed error ratios $3.9 \approx 4$ and $8.1 \approx 8$ under halving of $\eta$ (Section~\ref{sec:certificate}).
\end{remark}

The value correction becomes a quote correction by one more envelope evaluation. Combining \eqref{eq:envelope} with the first-order condition $m^\ast - K = 1/h(m^\ast)$ gives $m^\ast_\eta(K) = K + 1/\Phi_\eta'(K)$, and the exact strikes expand as $K^{\pm}_\eta(x) = K^{\pm}_0(x) + \eta\,\mathrm{D}^{\pm}\nu_1(x) + O(\eta^2)$, so:

\begin{corollary}[The tangent policy]\label{cor:tangentpolicy}
On interior states, the optimal quotes of the deformed problem satisfy
\[
m^{\ast,\pm}_\eta(x) \;=\; K^{\pm}_0(x) + \frac1h
\;+\; \eta\,\Big[\, \mathrm{D}^{\pm}\nu_1(x) \;-\; \frac{\psi'\big(K^{\pm}_0(x)\big)}{h^{2}} \,\Big] \;+\; O(\eta^2).
\]
The first-order quote correction decomposes into the inventory-value response $\mathrm{D}^{\pm}\nu_1$ and the direct markup-distribution response $-\psi'/h^2$; the two are computable separately, from one linear solve and one derivative evaluation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The curvature amplification factor]\label{rem:resolvent}
From \eqref{eq:firstorder}, $\|\nu_1\| \le \|L_0^{-1}\|\,\|R_\psi\|$. The forcing is bounded by the curvature through Corollary~\ref{cor:oscbound}-type estimates, but the resolvent norm belongs to the anchor: it is governed by the spectral gap of the anchor inventory birth--death dynamics, and it grows as the anchor problem approaches a low-carry or weakly mean-reverting regime. Small uncertainty in win-curve curvature therefore need not imply small uncertainty in policy when the inventory dynamics are near instability; $\|L_0^{-1}\|$ is the policy's curvature amplification factor, and it is the quantitative bridge between this paper and robust policy learning built on it.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The robustness remark of \citet{cotton2026skew} is the first shadow]
\citet{cotton2026skew} reported numerically that the error in the zero-inventory skew under a slowly varying hazard is about a quarter of the hazard's relative variation across the visited strikes, shrinking linearly. In the present language that experiment perturbs $\Phi$ by a specific near-quadratic $\psi$ and reads one component of $\eta\nu_1$; the observed constant is a functional of $\psi$ computable from \eqref{eq:firstorder}. The robustness statement is thereby upgraded from an error bound to the leading term of a convergent expansion.
\end{remark}

\section{From distributions to deformations}\label{sec:envelope}

The hierarchy consumes deformations of the \emph{effective log-value}; named distribution families are specified by deformations of the \emph{log-survival curve}. The envelope theorem converts one to the other without recomputing the optimal markup.

\begin{proposition}[Envelope transfer]\label{prop:envelope}
Let $-\log \Fbar_\theta(m) = hm + \sum_j \theta_j\, r_j(m)$ with the optimizer of $(m-K)\Fbar_\theta(m)$ unique and interior on the strike range of interest. Then, with $m_0(K) = K + \tfrac1h$ the exponential optimum,
\begin{equation}
\Phi_\theta(K) \;=\; \Phi_0(K) + \sum_j \theta_j\, r_j\big(m_0(K)\big)
\;-\; \frac{1}{2h^2}\sum_{j,k}\theta_j\theta_k\, r_j'\big(m_0(K)\big)\, r_k'\big(m_0(K)\big) + O(\|\theta\|^3).
\label{eq:envelopetransfer}
\end{equation}
To first order the movement of the optimizer drops out: the deformation of the log-value is the deformation of the log-survival curve evaluated at the exponential optimum. If the family is itself nonlinear in $\theta$, $-\log\Fbar_\theta = hm + \theta r(m) + \theta^2 r_2(m) + \cdots$, the family's own curvature adds $\theta^2 r_2(m_0(K))$ to \eqref{eq:envelopetransfer} at second order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $g(m;\theta) = \log(m-K) - hm - \sum_j\theta_j r_j(m)$, so $\log G_\theta(K) = g(m^\ast(\theta);\theta)$. By the envelope theorem $\partial_{\theta_j}\log G_\theta = -r_j(m^\ast)$, which at $\theta = 0$ is $-r_j(m_0)$. For the second order, differentiate the first-order condition $\tfrac{1}{m-K} - h - \sum\theta_j r_j'(m) = 0$: with $\partial^2_m g\big|_0 = -h^2$, one gets $\partial_{\theta_k} m^\ast\big|_0 = -r_k'(m_0)/h^2$, hence $\partial^2_{\theta_j\theta_k}\log G\big|_0 = -r_j'(m_0)\,\partial_{\theta_k}m^\ast\big|_0 = r_j'(m_0) r_k'(m_0)/h^2$. Change sign for $\Phi = -\log(G/G_\star)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Weibull example]\label{cor:weibull}
For $\Fbar_k(m) = e^{-(hm)^k}$ with $k = 1 + \eta$, the expansion $(hm)^{1+\eta} = hm + \eta\, hm\log(hm) + \tfrac{\eta^2}{2} hm \log^2(hm) + \cdots$ gives $r(m) = hm\log(hm)$ and hence the effective deformation
\[
\psi(K) \;=\; (1 + hK)\,\log(1 + hK),
\]
ready for insertion into \eqref{eq:firstorder}.
\end{corollary}

The hierarchy perturbs through effective log-values, and one should ask when a proposed $\Phi$ corresponds to any markup distribution at all. The question is a rationalizability question of the kind familiar from producer theory, where indirect profit functions determine supply through the envelope; the modern converse direction is \citet{sinander2022}. The envelope identities answer it locally here.

\begin{proposition}[Local admissibility]\label{prop:admissible}
Let $\Phi$ be $C^2$ on an interval $I$ with, on $I$,
\[
\Phi'(K) > 0, \qquad \Phi''(K) < \Phi'(K)^2, \qquad G(K)\,\Phi'(K) \le 1 .
\]
Define $m^\ast(K) = K + 1/\Phi'(K)$ and $\Fbar(m^\ast(K)) = G(K)\,\Phi'(K)$. Then $m^\ast$ is strictly increasing on $I$, $G$ is strictly convex, $\Fbar$ is a well-defined strictly decreasing function with values in $(0,1]$ on $m^\ast(I)$, and for every strike $\widetilde K$ with $m^\ast(\widetilde K)$ interior to $m^\ast(I)$ the program $\sup_m (m - \widetilde K)\,\Fbar(m)$ over $m^\ast(I)$ has the unique interior optimum $m^\ast(\widetilde K)$ with value $G(\widetilde K)$. In particular, sufficiently small $C^2$ deformations of the affine anchor remain admissible on any compact strike interval.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$dm^\ast/dK = 1 - \Phi''/\Phi'^2 > 0$ and, from $G' = -G\Phi'$, $G'' = G(\Phi'^2 - \Phi'') > 0$; also $\tfrac{d}{dK}[G\Phi'] = G(\Phi'' - \Phi'^2) < 0$, so $\Fbar$ is strictly decreasing along the strictly increasing curve $m^\ast$, with values in $(0,1]$ by hypothesis. The hazard of $\Fbar$ along the curve is
\[
h_{\Fbar}\big(m^\ast(K)\big) = -\frac{d}{dm}\log\Fbar
= \frac{-\tfrac{d}{dK}\log\big[G(K)\Phi'(K)\big]}{dm^\ast/dK}
= \frac{\Phi' - \Phi''/\Phi'}{\,1 - \Phi''/\Phi'^2\,} = \Phi'(K),
\]
so the first-order condition $1/(m - \widetilde K) = h_{\Fbar}(m)$ holds at $m = m^\ast(\widetilde K)$, with value $(1/\Phi')\cdot G\Phi' = G(\widetilde K)$. For uniqueness, differentiate the hazard along the curve: $h_{\Fbar}'(m) = \Phi''\,/\,(1 - \Phi''/\Phi'^2)$, and
\[
h_{\Fbar}'(m) + h_{\Fbar}(m)^2 \;=\; \frac{\Phi''\,\Phi'^2 + \Phi'^2\big(\Phi'^2 - \Phi''\big)}{\Phi'^2 - \Phi''}
\;=\; \frac{\Phi'^4}{\Phi'^2 - \Phi''} \;>\; 0 .
\]
At any stationary point of $\log[(m-\widetilde K)\Fbar(m)]$ one has $1/(m-\widetilde K) = h_{\Fbar}(m)$, so the second derivative there is $-1/(m-\widetilde K)^2 - h_{\Fbar}'(m) = -\big(h_{\Fbar}^2 + h_{\Fbar}'\big) < 0$: every stationary point is a strict local maximum, hence there is exactly one on $m^\ast(I)$. The final statement holds because all three inequalities are strict and open for the affine anchor on a compact interval.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A response library, not one calculation per family]
Expand a log-survival deformation on a basis $\{r_j\}$ --- splines, Chebyshev polynomials, or hazard bumps, on the compact strike interval the policy actually visits --- and precompute the responses $u_j = -L_0^{-1}R_{\psi_j}$ once, at the exponential anchor. Then
\[
\nu(\theta) \;=\; \nu_0 + \sum_j \theta_j\, u_j + \tfrac12\sum_{j,k}\theta_j\theta_k\, u_{jk} + \cdots
\]
reprices Weibull shape perturbations, Gompertz hazards, gamma and lognormal displacement families, log-logistic and bounded-support curves, mixtures, and empirically estimated survival curves \citep{fgp2017} from one factorization of one matrix, for the fixed anchor problem.

Two qualifications keep the claim precise. Some of these families contain the exponential as a native parameter limit (Weibull, gamma, Gompertz) and are reached along their own parameter homotopy; others are reached through a basis approximation of their log-survival difference on the visited interval, with the approximation error entering alongside the truncation error. And Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope} is local, conditional on a unique interior optimizer throughout the range traversed. Within those bounds the theory is a functional expansion of the log-survival curve; the named families merely select coordinates $\theta$. If the splines are $C^2$ rather than analytic, Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy} applies in its $C^r$ form, through second order.
\end{remark}

\section{The distribution-free parity theorem}\label{sec:parity}

\begin{theorem}[Parity]\label{thm:parity}
Suppose the buy and sell sides face the same enquiry-value curve $G$, the carrying cost is even, $c(-x) = c(x)$, and the consistency equation \eqref{eq:consistency} (with any reflection-symmetric boundary closure) has a locally unique solution branch depending smoothly on $\ell$. Then
\[
\nu_{1-q}(x) \;=\; \nu_q(-x) \qquad\text{(after the normalization $\nu(0)=0$)},
\]
so the zero-inventory skew contains only odd and the zero-inventory convexity only even powers of the log-odds:
\[
S_q(0) = d_1 \ell + d_3 \ell^3 + \cdots, \qquad
C_q(0) - C_{1/2}(0) = g_2 \ell^2 + g_4 \ell^4 + \cdots .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\nu$ solve the problem at imbalance $q$ and set $\tilde\nu(x) = \nu(-x)$. Then $\mathrm{D}^{\pm}\tilde\nu(x) = \mathrm{D}^{\mp}\nu(-x)$, so the strikes swap sides at the reflected inventory, and since $c$ is even, $\tilde\nu$ satisfies \eqref{eq:consistency} with $q$ replaced by $1-q$. Local uniqueness identifies $\tilde\nu$ with $\nu_{1-q}$. Then $S_{1-q}(0) = -S_q(0)$ and $C_{1-q}(0) = C_q(0)$; since $q \mapsto 1-q$ is $\ell \mapsto -\ell$, smoothness in $\ell$ gives the stated power series.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Parity versus leading order]
Symmetry alone does not make $d_1$ or $g_2$ nonzero, nor sign the width response: in principle a particular curve could have cubic-leading skew or quartic-leading widening. What the theorem establishes is the parity; the familiar reading --- skew first order in imbalance, width second order --- holds under the nondegeneracy $d_1 \neq 0$, $g_2 \neq 0$, which the exponential family satisfies with the all-orders coefficients
\[
\delta = \frac{\ell}{h}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{\log\cosh\ell}{h},
\]
and which the certificate's strongly curved test curve also exhibits, with $d_1 \approx 0.797/h$ against the exponential $1/h$ and $g_2 \approx 0.019 > 0$. Exponentiality supplies coefficients; parity is universal; nondegeneracy sits in between, generic but not automatic.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical checks}\label{sec:certificate}

A single seventeen-state experiment checks instances, not theorems. What the accompanying script \texttt{verify\_normal\_form.py} provides is an independent check of every formula and every predicted asymptotic order, on the lattice formulation of the certificate of \citet{cotton2026skew}: quadratic cost, $q = 0.6$, the third-difference closure of Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}, and the Weibull-born deformation $\psi(K) = (1+hK)\log(1+hK)$. It verifies:
\begin{itemize}
\item the normal form \eqref{eq:normalform} and translation form \eqref{eq:translation} on randomized $(A, S, q)$ at relative error $\sim 10^{-16}$, for a deformation far from exponential ($\eta = 0.35$), and the gauge property $G(T_\lambda(K)) = \lambda\,G(K)$ at $\sim 10^{-15}$;
\item the affine defect $D \equiv 2\epsilon + 2\gamma$ at $\sim 10^{-16}$, and for curved $\Phi$ the expansions \eqref{eq:Tcexpansion}--\eqref{eq:defectexpansion} with $O(\eta^2)$ remainders (ratios $3.6$--$3.8 \approx 4$ under halving), with the measured oscillation inside the bound of Corollary~\ref{cor:oscbound} by a factor of about two;
\item the hierarchy of Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}: $\|\nu_\eta - \nu_0 - \eta\nu_1\|_\infty$ shrinks at ratio $3.9 \approx 4$ and $\|\nu_\eta - \nu_0 - \eta\nu_1 - \eta^2\nu_2\|_\infty$ at ratio $8.1 \approx 8$; the resolvent inequality of Remark~\ref{rem:resolvent} holds with amplification factor $\|L_0^{-1}\|_\infty \approx 73$ on the test anchor;
\item the envelope transfer \eqref{eq:envelopetransfer} against direct optimization of the true Weibull families: first order at ratio $\approx 4$, second order (including the family's own curvature term) at ratio $\approx 8$;
\item the rigidity boundary of Theorem~\ref{thm:hamrigidity} in both directions: the affine-exponential family balances rigidly, and satisfies $2q\,G'(K) = G'(K + \gamma - \delta)$, at $\sim 10^{-16}$, while the curved test curve's balancing gap, \emph{minimized over all translation pairs}, stays above $10^{-2}$;
\item the admissibility construction of Proposition~\ref{prop:admissible}, with the reconstructed survival curve re-optimizing to $G$ at $\sim 10^{-16}$, and the tangent policy of Corollary~\ref{cor:tangentpolicy} at ratio $3.9 \approx 4$;
\item the parity of Theorem~\ref{thm:parity}: $|\nu_{1-q}(x) - \nu_q(-x)| \sim 10^{-15}$, with coefficients $d_1 \to 0.797$ and $g_2 \to 0.019$ stable under $\ell \to \ell/2$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Relation to earlier work and the program}\label{sec:relation}

\citet{cotton2026skew} proved the exact compression under exponentiality and remarked that the assumption is needed only locally; the present paper is that remark grown into a theory, with the exponential case recast as the integrable anchor of a normal form rather than a fortunate special case.

\citet{cotton2026finite} plays the analogous role in the transient Avellaneda--Stoikov line \citep{avellaneda2008,glft2012}, where the exponential intensity curve is what linearizes the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation in the first place; its concluding problem --- a finite-horizon robustness statement for locally exponential intensity curves --- is now posed correctly as the finite-horizon instance of this paper's hierarchy, with the tilted terminal condition joining the forcing terms.

The nearest existing literature treats general intensity curves and asymmetric sides numerically or through general Hamiltonians without structural reductions. \citet{gueantlehalle2015} is the canonical treatment of general intensity shapes, on the execution side; \citet{gueant2017} allows fully general asymmetric intensities and characterizes quotes through an implicit hazard-type condition solved through the HJB system; \citet{bergaultgueant2021} does the same at scale for OTC market makers; \citet{campizabal2020} handles general intensities under partial information via viscosity solutions. None states a structural reduction of the asymmetric problem, which is what the normal form and its boundary provide.

The perturbation genre around solvable market-making models expands in scalar parameters \citep{bergaultevgv2021}; the hierarchy here deforms the win curve as a function. Among contemporaneous work, \citet{barzykinciceri2026} works with a general fill-probability curve and exact quote-map inversion but without symmetry structure, and \citet{bbgl2025} obtains first-order corrections in effect-strength parameters for adverse selection and price reading.

The algebraic ancestry is the same as in those papers: the identity behind Theorem~\ref{thm:normalform} is the Cram\'er--Chernoff exponential tilt of an asymmetric walk \citep{chernoff1952,feller1968}, and the affine case's rigid translation is the birth--death symmetrization known since \citet{ledermannreuter1954} and \citet{karlinmcgregor1957}. The envelope argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:envelope} is the standard machinery of \citet{milgromsegal2002}; the win-curve estimation that would supply empirical $\theta$ coordinates is the econometrics of \citet{fgp2017}.

What is new here is the geometry above that algebra. For arbitrary enquiry-value curves the tilt acts as an exact translation in the log-value coordinates, and rigid removal is possible exactly on the affine-exponential family, with pure exponentiality recovered from tails (Theorems~\ref{thm:rigidity} and~\ref{thm:hamrigidity}). The failure to lift the pointwise gauge to a potential is a curvature functional with the widening of \citet{cotton2026skew} as its integrable value and an explicit oscillation bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:defect}, Corollary~\ref{cor:oscbound}). The correction, to the potential and to the quotes, is a hierarchy driven by one bordered-tridiagonal operator with envelope-transferred, admissibility-checked forcing (Theorem~\ref{thm:hierarchy}, Corollary~\ref{cor:tangentpolicy}, Propositions~\ref{prop:envelope} and~\ref{prop:admissible}). The parity theorem, finally, marks the boundary between what imbalance does to a dealer because of arithmetic --- oddness and evenness, which no model in the class can evade --- and what it does because the market's rejection curve happens to be memoryless.

Two objects built here are the intended interface to sequels: the tangent policy of Corollary~\ref{cor:tangentpolicy}, which tells a learning system what the first-order-correct quotes are rather than only what the corrected value function is, and the curvature amplification factor of Remark~\ref{rem:resolvent}, which prices how win-curve uncertainty propagates to policy uncertainty through the anchor's spectral gap. A robust controller assembled from the exponential anchor, the tangent responses, and a learned residual is developed separately.
