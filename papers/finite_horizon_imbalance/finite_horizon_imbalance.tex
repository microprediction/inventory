\begin{abstract}
In the exponential-intensity market making model of \citet{glft2012}, the balanced and the imbalanced problem both reduce to finite linear systems. In the balanced case the reduced matrix is symmetric; with asymmetric side scales $A^b \neq A^a$ it is not manifestly so, and the case is normally treated numerically. We show that the asymmetric system is exactly diagonally similar to a symmetric Jacobi system at the geometric-mean activity, with an imbalance-dependent exponential tilt in the terminal condition. This yields an exact real spectral representation for every imbalance. The terminal tilt is load-bearing: relative to the ordinary balanced problem, the flow-induced displacement of the quoted mid is horizon-dependent, vanishing at maturity and converging to the steady-state constant $-\delta$ with error bounded at the spectral-gap rate, with $\delta = \tfrac{1}{2k}\log(A^b/A^a)$, while the spread exhibits a finite-horizon transient that is second order in the imbalance at zero inventory. The steady-state equivalence of \citet{cotton2026skew} is the long-horizon limit. An exact all-horizon constant shift does hold, but for a modified problem: the dealer who marks her terminal inventory at the flow-adjusted price $S_T - \delta$ is, at every horizon, exactly the balanced dealer of the slower market. The exponential intensity curve with a common decay $k$ is essential to the reduction.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} market making, order imbalance, skew, finite horizon, birth--death processes, Avellaneda--Stoikov.

\section{Introduction}

\citet{avellaneda2008} posed the modern market making problem, and \citet{glft2012} solved it: with exponential fill intensities $\lambda(\delta) = A e^{-k\delta}$ and symmetric buy and sell scales, the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation reduces to a linear system of ordinary differential equations indexed by inventory. The symmetry of the scales is essential to the symmetry of the reduced matrix as conventionally presented; the linear reduction itself survives unequal scales. When customers are more often sellers than buyers the scales differ; \citet{gueant2017} develops closed-form approximations for this and more general cases, and the exact quotes are computed numerically \citep{gueant2017,bergaultgueant2021}.

This note makes a single observation and develops its consequence, including the consequence it does not have. The imbalanced linear system is diagonally conjugate to a symmetric system at the geometric-mean activity. The conjugation is exact, spectrum included, so the asymmetric finite-horizon problem acquires an exact spectral representation. But the conjugation carries the imbalance into the terminal condition as an exponential tilt, and the tilt does not commute with the horizon: at maturity it exactly cancels the flow skew, and only as the horizon grows does the displacement of the quoted mid converge to the steady-state constant $-\delta$ of \citet{cotton2026skew}. The finite-horizon story is a term structure, not a constant.

The exponential intensity curve, with a common decay $k$ on both sides, is what linearizes the HJB in the first place. General intensity curves have no such reduction; side-dependent decays $k^b \neq k^a$ prevent the single-power linearization used here. Within the model the results are exact on interior inventory states and certified to machine precision by an accompanying script, which also verifies the terminal-time counterexample that rules out the stronger claim.

\section{The reduced system with imbalanced flow}\label{sec:model}

Following \citet{glft2012}, a dealer quotes a bid $S - \delta^b$ and an ask $S + \delta^a$ around a mid $S$ with $dS = \sigma\, dW$. Fills arrive with intensities $\lambda^b(\delta^b) = A^b e^{-k\delta^b}$ and $\lambda^a(\delta^a) = A^a e^{-k\delta^a}$, moving the inventory $q$ within bounds $|q| \le Q$. The dealer has CARA utility with risk aversion $\gamma$, horizon $T$, and liquidates terminal inventory at $S_T$; the admissible quote processes are those of \citet{glft2012}. Throughout, $Q \ge 1$ and $A^a, A^b, k, \gamma, \sigma > 0$. The only departure from \citet{glft2012} is $A^b \neq A^a$: sellers and buyers arrive at different rates, in constant ratio.

The change of variables of \citet{glft2012} survives the asymmetry, because the side scales enter only through constants. Write $u(t,x,q,S) = -\exp(-\gamma(x+qS))\, v_q(t)^{-\gamma/k}$ and the HJB collapses to the linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:system}
\dot v_q(t) \;=\; \alpha q^2\, v_q(t) \;-\; \eta^b\, v_{q+1}(t) \;-\; \eta^a\, v_{q-1}(t),
\qquad v_q(T) = 1,
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = \tfrac{k\gamma\sigma^2}{2}$, $\eta^s = A^s\big(1+\tfrac{\gamma}{k}\big)^{-(1+k/\gamma)}$ for $s \in \{a,b\}$, and the missing neighbor terms dropped at $q = \pm Q$. Writing $c_0 = \tfrac{1}{\gamma}\log(1+\tfrac{\gamma}{k})$, the optimal quote depths are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotes}
\delta^b(t,q) = \frac{1}{k}\log\frac{v_q(t)}{v_{q+1}(t)} + c_0 \quad (q < Q),
\qquad
\delta^a(t,q) = \frac{1}{k}\log\frac{v_q(t)}{v_{q-1}(t)} + c_0 \quad (q > -Q).
\end{equation}
In matrix form $\dot v = Mv$ where $M$ is tridiagonal with diagonal $\alpha q^2$ and off-diagonals $-\eta^b$ above and $-\eta^a$ below. Since $-M$ is a Metzler matrix and the coupling graph is irreducible, $v(t) = e^{-M(T-t)}\mathbf{1}$ is strictly positive for $t < T$ and the logarithms in \eqref{eq:quotes} are well defined. The matrix is asymmetric exactly when the flow is.

\section{The symmetrization and what it does and does not give}\label{sec:theorem}

We state the general fact first.

\begin{lemma}[Any imbalance profile symmetrizes]\label{lem:general}
Let $M$ be tridiagonal with arbitrary real diagonal and off-diagonal entries $-\eta^b_q$ (coupling $q$ to $q+1$) and $-\eta^a_q$ (coupling $q$ to $q-1$), all $\eta$ positive and possibly inventory-dependent. Define $d_{q+1}/d_q = \sqrt{\eta^b_q/\eta^a_{q+1}}$ and $D = \mathrm{diag}(d_q)$. Then $DMD^{-1}$ is symmetric, with both off-diagonals between levels $q$ and $q+1$ equal to $-\sqrt{\eta^b_q\, \eta^a_{q+1}}$. In particular the spectrum of $M$ is real and, the couplings being strictly positive, simple.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$(DMD^{-1})_{q,q+1} = -\eta^b_q\, d_q/d_{q+1} = -\sqrt{\eta^b_q \eta^a_{q+1}}$ and $(DMD^{-1})_{q+1,q} = -\eta^a_{q+1}\, d_{q+1}/d_q = -\sqrt{\eta^b_q \eta^a_{q+1}}$. Boundary rows have no missing counterpart to disturb. A symmetric Jacobi matrix with nonzero off-diagonals has real simple eigenvalues.
\end{proof}

For constant scales the conjugation is a geometric tilt. Let $r = \sqrt{\eta^b/\eta^a} = \sqrt{A^b/A^a}$, $\bar\eta = \sqrt{\eta^b\eta^a}$, $\delta = \tfrac{1}{k}\log r = \tfrac{1}{2k}\log(A^b/A^a)$, and $\tau = T - t$.

\begin{theorem}[Conjugacy, with the imbalance in the terminal condition]\label{thm:conj}
Let $w_q(t) = r^{q} v_q(t)$. Then $w$ solves the symmetric-dynamics system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balanced}
\dot w_q(t) \;=\; \alpha q^2\, w_q(t) \;-\; \bar\eta\,\big(w_{q+1}(t) + w_{q-1}(t)\big),
\qquad w_q(T) = r^{q},
\end{equation}
and, writing $\delta^s_{w}$ for the depths computed from $w$ via \eqref{eq:quotes},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shift}
\delta^b_{\mathrm{imb}}(t,q) = \delta^b_{w}(t,q) + \delta,
\qquad
\delta^a_{\mathrm{imb}}(t,q) = \delta^a_{w}(t,q) - \delta .
\end{equation}
The comparator in \eqref{eq:shift} is the \emph{tilted}-terminal problem, not the ordinary balanced problem: unless $r=1$, $w$ is not proportional to the balanced solution $z(t) = e^{-\bar M (T-t)}\mathbf{1}$ at finite horizon, where $\bar M$ denotes the symmetric matrix of \eqref{eq:balanced}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:general} with constant scales gives $d_q = r^q$, common off-diagonal $\bar\eta$, and $w_q(T) = r^q v_q(T) = r^q$. The quote relations follow from $\log(v_q/v_{q\pm1}) = \log(w_q/w_{q\pm1}) \pm \log r$. For the last statement, $w(T) = r^{\cdot}$ and $z(T) = \mathbf{1}$ are not proportional unless $r = 1$, and $e^{-\bar M \tau}$ is invertible.
\end{proof}

The terminal tilt has an exact economic meaning, and the sign matters. With $w_q = r^q v_q$ and $r = e^{k\delta}$,
\[
-\exp\big(-\gamma(x+qS)\big)\, v_q^{-\gamma/k} \;=\; -\exp\big(-\gamma(x+q(S-\delta))\big)\, w_q^{-\gamma/k}:
\]
in the $w$ variables the reference price is $S - \delta$. The asymmetric arrival curves $A^s e^{-k\delta^s}$ are balanced around $\bar S = S - \delta$, while terminal inventory is liquidated at $S_T = \bar S_T + \delta$. The mismatch between the price the flow is balanced around and the price the book is marked at is what generates everything horizon-dependent below.

\begin{theorem}[The term structure of the flow displacement]\label{thm:term}
Let $z(\tau) = e^{-\bar M \tau}\mathbf{1}$ and $w(\tau) = e^{-\bar M \tau} r^{\cdot}$, and let $\delta^s_{\mathrm{std}}$ denote the ordinary balanced quotes computed from $z$. For interior inventory $-Q < q < Q$, the displacement of the quote-price midpoint $m = S + \tfrac12(\delta^a - \delta^b)$ and the spread difference relative to the ordinary balanced problem are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dm}
\Delta m_q(\tau) \;=\; -\delta \;+\; \frac{1}{2k}\log\frac{w_{q+1}(\tau)\, z_{q-1}(\tau)}{w_{q-1}(\tau)\, z_{q+1}(\tau)},
\qquad
\Delta s_q(\tau) \;=\; \frac{1}{k}\log\frac{w_q(\tau)^2\, z_{q+1}(\tau)\, z_{q-1}(\tau)}{w_{q+1}(\tau)\, w_{q-1}(\tau)\, z_q(\tau)^2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\Delta m_q(0) = 0$ and $\Delta s_q(0) = 0$: at maturity the terminal condition cancels the flow skew entirely, and both quote depths tend to $c_0$ regardless of $A^b/A^a$. As $\tau \to \infty$ with $\bar\eta$ fixed, both $w$ and $z$ align with the positive ground-state eigenvector of $\bar M$, so
\[
\Delta m_q(\tau) \longrightarrow -\delta, \qquad \Delta s_q(\tau) \longrightarrow 0,
\]
with error bounded at the spectral-gap rate, $O(e^{-(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)\tau})$. When sellers dominate ($A^b > A^a$, $\delta > 0$) both quoted prices sit below their balanced counterparts in the long-horizon limit. The increment of the depth skew $\tfrac12(\delta^b - \delta^a)$ over its balanced counterpart tends to $+\delta$; at $q = 0$, where the balanced skew vanishes, this is also the total depth skew.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the standard-balanced depths from \eqref{eq:shift} using $\delta^b_{w} - \delta^b_{\mathrm{std}} = \tfrac1k \log(w_q z_{q+1}/(w_{q+1} z_q))$ and its ask counterpart, then form the mid and spread combinations. At $\tau = 0$, $w = r^{\cdot}$ and $z = \mathbf{1}$ give $\tfrac{1}{2k}\log r^2 = \delta$ in \eqref{eq:dm}, cancelling $-\delta$; the spread ratio is $1$. As $\tau \to \infty$, $w(\tau) \sim (r^{\cdot}, \varphi)\, e^{-\lambda_1 \tau} \varphi$ and $z(\tau) \sim (\mathbf{1}, \varphi)\, e^{-\lambda_1 \tau} \varphi$ for the simple ground pair $(\lambda_1, \varphi)$ of $\bar M$ with $\varphi > 0$, so all component ratios converge to ratios of $\varphi$ and the logarithms in \eqref{eq:dm} tend to $0$; the error is $O(e^{-(\lambda_2 - \lambda_1)\tau})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Numerical anchor]\label{cor:counter}
For $Q=2$, $\alpha = 0.1$, $\eta^b = 2$, $\eta^a = \tfrac12$, $k=1$, $\gamma = 0.1$, $\tau = 1$, at inventory $q = 0$: the ordinary balanced problem at $\bar\eta = 1$ has both depths $1.109623$; the imbalanced problem has bid depth $1.435402$, ask depth $0.818182$, mid displacement $\Delta m_0 = -0.308610$, spread $2.253584$, and spread difference $\Delta s_0 = 0.034338$. The steady-state shift is $-\delta = -0.693147$. An all-horizon constant displacement is therefore false for the standard terminal condition; the certificate reproduces these numbers to machine precision.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Exact spectral representation]\label{cor:closed}
For constant coefficients, with $\bar M = D M D^{-1} = U \Lambda U^\top$,
\[
v(t) \;=\; D^{-1} U e^{-\Lambda (T-t)} U^\top D\, \mathbf{1},
\]
one real spectral decomposition per geometric-mean activity: $\bar M$ depends on the scales through $\bar\eta$, so the decomposition is reusable across imbalance ratios only while $A^b A^a$ is held fixed. The balanced solution of \citet{glft2012} is the case $r=1$. Time-dependent scales in constant ratio preserve the conjugation but not this formula: $\bar M(t)$ at different times need not commute, and a time-ordered exponential or direct integration replaces the eigendecomposition. A time-varying ratio adds the diagonal term $q\, \tfrac{d}{dt}\log r(t)$ to the transformed system and is not captured by a time-varying $\delta$ alone.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[The all-horizon shift holds under flow-adjusted terminal marking]\label{cor:salvage}
Suppose the dealer marks terminal inventory at $S_T - \delta$ instead of $S_T$. Then $v_q(T) = r^{-q}$, hence $w_q(T) = \mathbf{1}$: the transformed problem is exactly the ordinary balanced problem, and
\[
\delta^b_{\mathrm{imb}}(t,q) = \delta^b_{\mathrm{std}}(t,q) + \delta,
\qquad
\delta^a_{\mathrm{imb}}(t,q) = \delta^a_{\mathrm{std}}(t,q) - \delta
\qquad \text{for all } t, \; -Q < q < Q:
\]
constant mid displacement $-\delta$ and identical spread at every horizon. The dealer who marks her book at the price her flow is balanced around is, at every horizon, the balanced dealer of the slower market.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Parity of the spread response]\label{cor:parity}
Fix $\bar\eta$ and write $r = e^{\varepsilon}$. Reflection of inventory maps $(q, \varepsilon) \mapsto (-q, -\varepsilon)$ and leaves the system invariant, so the spread satisfies $s_q(\varepsilon) = s_{-q}(-\varepsilon)$. At $q = 0$ the spread is therefore even in $\varepsilon$: it has no linear response, and the response is $O(\varepsilon^2)$, with a quadratic coefficient that is nonzero at generic intermediate horizons and vanishes at $\tau = 0$ and in the long-horizon limit. At $q \neq 0$ a linear term is generically present at intermediate horizons. The mid displacement at $q=0$ is odd in $\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The algebra, again]
The conjugation is the same birth--death symmetrization used in \citet{cotton2026skew}, with provenance in the spectral theory of birth and death processes \citep{karlinmcgregor1957} and, in random-walk form, in the tilt factors of first-passage theory \citep{feller1968}. Lemma~\ref{lem:general} is the standard fact that a Jacobi matrix with positive couplings is diagonally symmetrizable. The contribution is the destination and its boundary: an exact symmetrization and spectral representation for the common-$k$ exponential asymmetric-scale finite-horizon problem, together with the exact term structure \eqref{eq:dm} that the terminal condition imposes.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The exponential assumption]
The reduction \eqref{eq:system}, and hence everything here, requires the exponential intensity curve with a common decay $k$. Side-dependent decays $k^b \neq k^a$ do not merely break the constant shift: no single change of variables of the GLFT form linearizes both sides at once, so there is no linear tridiagonal system for Lemma~\ref{lem:general} to act on. If quote depths are constrained to be nonnegative, \eqref{eq:quotes} must be clipped where the unconstrained optimizer is negative, and the exact reduction fails in those states. For the steady-state model \citet{cotton2026skew} shows the analogous exponential assumption is needed only locally, with quantified error; we expect, but do not prove here, a finite-horizon analogue.
\end{remark}

\section{Numerical certificate}\label{sec:certificate}

The claims are linear algebra and can be checked by machine. The script \texttt{verify\_finite\_horizon.py} verifies, to near machine precision: the conjugation of Lemma~\ref{lem:general} for randomized inventory-dependent intensities; the identities $v_q = r^{-q} w_q$ and \eqref{eq:shift} against the tilted comparator; the counterexample of Corollary~\ref{cor:counter} against the ordinary balanced comparator, reproducing the table; the term-structure formulas \eqref{eq:dm} against direct computation, with $\Delta m_q(0) = 0$ and the spectral-rate convergence $\Delta m_q(\tau) \to -\delta$; the flow-adjusted marking of Corollary~\ref{cor:salvage} giving the constant shift at every tested horizon; and the parity of Corollary~\ref{cor:parity}.

\section{Use}\label{sec:use}

Two objects survive for practice, both exact within the model. The spectral representation prices the imbalanced finite-horizon dealer with one symmetric eigendecomposition, for any imbalance and any horizon. And the term structure \eqref{eq:dm} is itself the falsifiable content: the flow-induced displacement of a dealer's mid should be near zero close to book close-out, should build toward $\tfrac{1}{2k}\log\tfrac{p}{1-p}$ in depth-skew increment terms, with $p$ the baseline seller share, as the effective horizon grows, and the spread should carry a finite-horizon transient, second order in the imbalance at flat inventory and of parameter-dependent sign. A desk that marks its terminal book at the flow-adjusted price recovers the constant shift of the steady-state theory exactly, at which point recalibrating the flow forecast at unchanged geometric-mean activity ($A^b A^a$ fixed) updates quotes by a constant and nothing else; a change in $A^b A^a$ also changes the balanced base problem, an effect that is second order near balance.

We leave to future work the finite-horizon robustness statement for locally exponential intensity curves, and the stochastic ratio, where the state-dependent tilt must be conjugated through the full generator: beyond the deterministic diagonal term of Corollary~\ref{cor:closed}, It\^o and possible cross-generator terms appear.
